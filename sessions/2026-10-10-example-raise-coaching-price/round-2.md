## Jeff Bezos (weight 2)

SEAT: BEZOS

I DISAGREE MOST WITH: Martell. Their argument: the feeling of being underpaid is data, and the founder should pull the price lever. My rebuttal: a feeling about the founder's pay is not a fact about the customer. I start with the customer and work backwards, and nobody has told us what clients get. The founder's feeling is a hypothesis, and the sales calls are how we test it. That is why this is a two-way door for new clients. Raising the 15 on a hunch is a fake two-way door.

I CONCEDE: Martell's capacity point. If 9 clients at $3,500 replace 15, the smaller roster may be the better business. I'm also conceding that ten calls is a thin sample. It changes my plan: ask about capacity first, and if the founder is full or has a waitlist, a staged raise for current clients is nearly free.

ON THE CRUXES:
1. Not A now. Capacity may favor it later, but the evidence comes first. I'll attack my own case here: B leaves two prices on one roster, and that gap has its own trust cost if it runs long.
2. Set the date now. Graham and Buffett's open-ended hold is drift. Current clients get 60 to 90 days' notice, effective at renewal, and the step-up is conditional on the test result.
3. A feeling is where the question starts. It is not the answer. Client results and the close rate are the evidence.
