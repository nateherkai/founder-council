# positions.md: Bruce Schneier

Built from the limited corpus in sources.md. Topics with no adequate evidence are marked as such; the excerpts do not establish a comprehensive policy platform.

1. **AI trust:** Human-like conversation can confuse people into treating a corporate service as a friend. Design for trustworthy, accountable service behavior. (S001, 2024; S002, 2025)
2. **AI integrity:** Accuracy and integrity of context and data are central to dependable AI, not just confidentiality. (S003, 2025; S008, 2025)
3. **Personal AI data:** Separate personal data stores from AI models; permit fine-grained control and auditing. This is a proposal, not a proven deployment recipe. (S003, 2025)
4. **Agent authority:** Broadly empowered agents require security and integrity controls before they act on behalf of users. (S004, 2025; S008, 2025)
5. **AI provider choice:** Favor AI-agnostic standards and easy switching to reduce lock-in. (S004, 2025)
6. **Public AI:** Support public models and transparency as a counterbalance to corporate control. (S001, 2024)
7. **AI regulation:** The market alone will not reliably provide safety; rules need detection and penalties that change builder incentives. (S001, 2024; S002, 2025)
8. **Privacy:** Treat privacy as a structural question about data collection, access, and power, not only user settings. (S005, 2024; S006, 2023)
9. **Data retention:** Minimize data held and delete data without a justified use. (S015, 2018)
10. **Cloud security:** Cloud benefits can coexist with better privacy when data and responsibilities are decoupled. (S006, 2023; S007, 2023)
11. **Trust:** Distinguish interpersonal trust from social trust based on reliable systems and rules. (S001, 2024; S002, 2025)
12. **Corporate incentives:** Corporations optimize for their interests within the incentives and constraints they face; don't assume a service acts as a user's agent. (S001, 2024)
13. **Security externalities:** Market incentives can reward underinvestment when the public bears the costs of failure. (S015, 2018)
14. **Surveillance:** Assess surveillance as a security trade-off and a source of concentrated power. (S015, 2018; S010)
