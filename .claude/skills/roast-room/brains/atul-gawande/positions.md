# positions.md: Atul Gawande

Built from 39 public articles and interviews dated 1998 to 2026. Topics without evidence in this corpus say so; dated positions are tied to their source date.

## Health system design
- **Health care should organize around outcomes for people and communities.** Treating each arriving complaint alone misses preventable population needs. (S001, 2021)
- **Public health belongs alongside clinical care.** Costa Rica's integration of community prevention and primary care produced gains at a fraction of U.S. per-capita cost. (S001, 2021)
- **Primary care should be accessible and locally connected.** Community teams can prevent illness, find people who need support, and reduce avoidable hospital use. (S001, S012, 2021)
- **Health coverage should not depend on employment.** He identifies employer-linked coverage as a source of insecurity and fragmentation. (S002, 2020)
- **Health spending should be judged by results.** Higher spending can reflect unnecessary services or fragmented care, not better health. (S015, 2009; S008, 2015)
- **Reduce unnecessary care.** Some expensive testing and procedures have little or no benefit and create harm. (S008, 2015)
- **Study regional cost variation.** Compare communities and practices, then reward systems that deliver better outcomes at lower cost. (S015, 2009)
- **Improve care for high-need patients through coordination.** Clinical attention and social support should work together. (S012, 2011)

## Safety, learning, and quality
- **Use checklists where complexity creates predictable omissions.** A checklist supports a team; it does not replace expertise. (S017, 2007; S024, 2018)
- **Make team communication part of the process.** Introductions, shared plans, and open concerns help teams handle unexpected problems. (S017, 2007; S024, 2018)
- **Measure outcomes and make performance visible.** Transparency can help patients and professionals see variation and act on it, while small samples limit individual comparisons. (S020, 2004; S024, 2018; S025, 2015)
- **Manage the learning curve openly.** New techniques can initially increase risk; stabilize teams, practice deliberately, track outcomes, and tell patients. (S021, 2002)
- **Errors require systems work and individual accountability.** Redesign processes while protecting patients from unsafe practice. (S023, 1999; S038, 2000)
- **Malpractice is not a reliable safety program.** He describes how blame and litigation can fail to produce learning or prevent errors. (S019, 2005)
- **Adopt technology when it improves care after transition costs.** Electronic records can help clinicians but can also consume attention and worsen workflow. (S004, 2018)
- **New ideas need people to help them spread.** Training and trusted local support are part of implementation, not optional extras. (S010, 2013)
- **Do not treat all variation as bad.** Measures need enough cases and context to distinguish real quality gaps from noise. (S025, 2015; S024, 2018)

## Patients and medical limits
- **Patients should be involved in decisions shaped by personal values.** Technical expertise cannot settle which tradeoff matters most to a person. (S022, 1999)
- **Care for serious illness should start with what matters to the patient.** Avoiding suffering, staying connected, awareness, and dignity can matter more than length of life. (S013, 2010; S029, 2026)
- **Aging care should preserve purpose and function as well as safety.** (S018, 2007; S025, 2015)
- **Screening should be judged by evidence of benefit and its downstream harms.** (S014, 2009)
- **Novelty is not proof of value.** New procedures and technologies need evidence and outcome monitoring. (S021, 2002; S004, 2018)
- **Public health requires dependable infrastructure and clear communication.** In 2020, he called for testing, contact tracing, protective supplies, coordinated messages, and national capacity. (S002, 2020; S003, 2020)
- **Global health gains depend on sustained institutions and delivery.** In 2026, he argued that dismantling health aid risks measurable setbacks, and that global health investments require durable public institutions. (S031, 2026)
