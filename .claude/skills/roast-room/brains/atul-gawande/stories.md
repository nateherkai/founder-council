# stories.md: Atul Gawande

Built from 39 public articles and interviews from 1998 to 2026. Each item names the lesson he draws or the system question the story raises.

1. **Costa Rica's health system:** A country with modest income improved life expectancy by pairing primary care with public health and focusing on preventable mortality. (S001)
2. **McAllen and El Paso:** Higher Medicare spending in McAllen did not yield better outcomes; variation exposed the role of local practice patterns and incentives. (S015)
3. **A surgical checklist across eight hospitals:** A concise routine helped surgical teams coordinate, catch omissions, and reduce complications. (S017, S025)
4. **The BetterBirth Project:** On-site coaching helped birth attendants practice a checklist and work through local obstacles to adoption. (S010)
5. **Camden's high-use patients:** A care team connected people with concentrated emergency use to medical and social support. The challenge is making the model effective and durable. (S012)
6. **The heart-surgery learning curve:** Stable teams, pre-briefs, debriefs, and tracked results helped one team improve faster; early practice still carried risk. (S021)
7. **The patient with a serious illness:** A conversation about what mattered to the patient changed the treatment goal from extending life at any cost to preserving a meaningful life. (S013)
8. **The electronic record transition:** A major software rollout cost far more in lost clinical capacity and implementation work than in software alone. A technology change is a workflow change. (S004)
9. **The nursing home and the garden:** Residents' need for purpose and relationships challenged the assumption that safety should override choice. (S018)
10. **A surgeon's deteriorating performance:** Repeated errors and unnecessary procedures showed how status, high volume, and weak accountability can put patients at risk. Structured evaluation offered both protection and a route to treatment. (S038)
11. **The history of forceps:** A useful tool was kept within one family for generations. Secrecy can delay diffusion of a life-saving practice. (S032)
12. **Morbidity and mortality review:** Publicly examining complications can turn individual failures into shared learning, if the institution supports honest discussion. (S023)
