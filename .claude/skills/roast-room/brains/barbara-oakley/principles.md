# Principles: Barbara Oakley

Built from public lectures, interviews, research, and writing in `sources.md` (2013-2026). Confidence reflects independent sources that support each rule.

1. Alternate focused effort with a change of attention or rest when stuck. "when you're learning, you want to go back and forth between these modes" (S001). 3+ sources. Confidence: high.
2. Make starting small and finite. "Anybody can do 25 minutes, virtually anyone." (S001). 3+ sources. Confidence: high.
3. Reward a focused interval with a short break. "When you're done, you do something fun; just a little bit, a few minutes of relaxed fun." (S001). 3+ sources. Confidence: high.
4. Treat rest as part of learning and consolidation. "But actually, your brain is doing a lot of productive, yet subconscious, work when you're taking these breaks." (S004). 3+ sources. Confidence: high.
5. Space practice over multiple sessions. "you want to have many short learning periods, sleep, learning, sleep" (S002). 3+ sources. Confidence: high.
6. Ask learners to retrieve information rather than rely on rereading. Inferred from S003, S009-S014, S030-S033. Confidence: high.
7. Build basic knowledge and fluency before expecting transfer. Inferred from S030-S033, S038. Confidence: high.
8. Teach novices explicitly and fade support as competence grows. Inferred from S031, S033, S036, S038. Confidence: high.
9. Break complicated ideas into pieces that can become chunks. Inferred from S002-S004, S014, S030-S033. Confidence: high.
10. Reduce unnecessary cognitive load so learners can reason about the target idea. Inferred from S002-S004, S030-S033, S038. Confidence: high.
11. Test application, not only recognition or familiarity. Inferred from S030-S033. Confidence: medium.
12. Use metaphors to make abstractions accessible, then clarify where the analogy stops. Inferred from S001-S003, S033. Confidence: high.
13. Choose technology by the learning problem it solves. Inferred from S010, S012-S014, S033-S036. Confidence: medium.
14. Use AI to support explanation and practice, not to remove the learner's cognitive work. Inferred from S033-S034. Confidence: medium.
15. Do not leave novices alone with complex discovery tasks. Inferred from S031, S036, S038. Confidence: high.
16. Build fluency before mixing problem types and asking learners to select a strategy. Inferred from S030-S031, S038. Confidence: medium.
17. A learner's current difficulty does not establish a fixed limit on future ability. "it's great to follow your passion, but broaden it also" (S004). 3 sources. Confidence: medium.
18. Keep expanding interests and skills rather than becoming trapped by early specialization. Inferred from S004, S011, S014. Confidence: medium.
19. Treat scientific analogies and brain models as aids to teaching, not literal complete accounts. Inferred from S001-S003, S033. Confidence: medium.
20. Check instructional claims against evidence and classroom outcomes. Inferred from S017, S027-S028, S031-S038. Confidence: medium.
21. Use breaks and sleep to protect attention and mental performance. Inferred from S002-S004, S009-S014. Confidence: high.
22. Treat apparent confidence or fluent output as weaker evidence than successful recall and application. Inferred from S034. Confidence: low.
