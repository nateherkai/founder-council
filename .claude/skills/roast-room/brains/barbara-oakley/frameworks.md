# Frameworks: Barbara Oakley

Built from public lectures, interviews, research, and writing listed in `sources.md` (2013-2026). The strongest evidence concerns learning and teaching.

## 1. Focused and diffuse modes

Switch between precise attention on familiar paths and relaxed thought that allows wider associations. When a learner is stuck, a brief change of attention or rest can help them return with a new path. Quote: "when you're learning, you want to go back and forth between these modes" (S001). Used in S001-S003, S004, S014.

## 2. Pomodoro Technique

Use a 25-minute focused interval with distractions off, followed by a short enjoyable break. The point is to make starting manageable and to practice focused attention; 25 minutes is a guideline, and continuing in flow is fine. Quote: "Anybody can do 25 minutes, virtually anyone." (S001). Used in S001-S004, S014.

## 3. Chunking

Practice component steps until they can be recalled and used together as one mental unit. Link the chunk to prior knowledge; that reduces the working-memory burden when solving more complex problems. Inferred from S002-S004, S014, S030-S033.

## 4. Retrieval practice

Make learners recall an idea or solve a problem without first looking at the explanation. Use questions and low-stakes tests to expose what is known and strengthen access. Inferred from S003, S009-S014, S030-S033.

## 5. Spaced practice and sleep

Return to material across short sessions and leave room for sleep and rest. Cramming feels fluent but provides fewer chances to consolidate and retrieve learning. Quote: "you want to have many short learning periods, sleep, learning, sleep" (S002). Used in S002-S004, S009-S014.

## 6. Interleaving and varied practice

After a learner has some fluency, mix examples and problem types so they must identify the method rather than repeat a cue. Use evidence of transfer, not familiarity, to judge mastery. Inferred from S030-S033, S038.

## 7. Worked examples and scaffold fading

Show a complete model, guide practice, then gradually remove supports as learners become fluent. For novices, clarity and enough background knowledge come before unguided discovery. Inferred from S031, S033, S036, S038.

## 8. Concrete to abstract

Start with an understandable case, model, or representation. Move gradually toward abstract symbols and independent problem solving, checking that each transition is secure. Inferred from S030-S031, S038.

## 9. Metaphor bridge

Use a vivid everyday analogy to make an unfamiliar process imaginable, then explicitly connect the analogy back to the real mechanism. A metaphor is a bridge, not the mechanism itself. Inferred from S001-S003, S010, S012-S014, S033, S038.

## 10. Cognitive load and working memory

Limit the number of new elements presented at once. Build foundational knowledge and chunks so working memory is available for reasoning. Inferred from S002-S004, S030-S033, S038.

## 11. Focus, break, reward

Turn an aversive task into a bounded start, focus on the work rather than the entire task, then take a small reward. Quote: "When you're done, you do something fun; just a little bit, a few minutes of relaxed fun." (S001). Used in S001-S004, S014.

## 12. Passion plus breadth

Interest is valuable, but early narrow specialization can constrain future options. Keep adding adjacent interests and skills. Quote: "it's great to follow your passion, but broaden it also" (S004). Used in S004, S011, S014.

## 13. Learning by teaching

Ask learners to explain an idea in their own words or teach it to another person. Explanation reveals gaps and organizes knowledge for retrieval. Inferred from S003, S009-S014, S030-S033.

## 14. AI as a practice aid

Use AI to generate questions, offer hints, or explain a concept, while preserving the learner's own retrieval and reasoning. Evaluate the learning goal and task before choosing a tool. Inferred from S010, S012-S014, S033-S036.

## 15. Evidence before educational novelty

Begin with the learning problem, then choose methods and tools that address it. Avoid treating neuroscience terms, technology, or fashionable pedagogy as evidence by themselves. Inferred from S017-S018, S027-S028, S031-S038.
