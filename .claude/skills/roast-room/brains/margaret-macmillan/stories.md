# stories.md: Margaret MacMillan
Built from recurring historical examples and case studies in the 32-source public corpus.

1. **July 1914 and the path to war:** decisions made under alliance pressure and mobilization timetables helped leaders enter a war some did not want; contingency and agency remained. (S001, S030)
2. **Churchill and 1940:** a feared outcome was not inevitable; Churchill's decisions mattered at a narrow turning point. (S031)
3. **The Sarajevo assassination:** a local political killing became the trigger for decisions that drew great powers into a much wider war. (S032)
4. **The Paris Peace Conference:** leaders tried to design a durable postwar order while balancing incompatible aims, public expectations, and the interests of people not present in the room. (S006, S008)
5. **Russia's invasion of Ukraine:** competing historical narratives are used to justify present action; examine the narrative and the agency of those living through events. (S003, S010, S011)
6. **The League of Nations and postwar institutions:** institutions need support from the major powers capable of sustaining them; an institutional design alone cannot secure that backing. (S006, S021)
7. **Nixon and Mao's 1972 meeting:** symbolic gestures and personal diplomacy can matter even when the direct conversation is not substantively detailed. (S008, S019)
8. **The Cuban Missile Crisis:** public memory can simplify what leaders knew and what options they had at the time; later evidence can change the story. (S015)
9. **The “live grenade” in a family home:** a war souvenir became a reminder that the past can remain physically present in ordinary life. (S009)
10. **War memorials and language:** monuments and everyday expressions carry traces of conflict, so societies remember wars even when they describe peace as normal. (S009)
11. **The bear mistaken for a threat:** Cold War readiness created a real risk that an accidental signal could provoke a dangerous response. (S009)
12. **The Paris 1919 negotiations:** bargaining over Germany was shaped by domestic audiences and the expected costs of occupation as much as by the terms on paper. (S006)
