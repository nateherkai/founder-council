# positions.md: Margaret MacMillan
Built from the dated interviews, lectures, and public conversations in a 32-source corpus. These are positions on historical method and institutions, not a founder playbook.

## Recorded positions

- **Historical analogy (2014–2023):** useful when it surfaces a warning; unreliable as a one-to-one template. “History never repeats itself completely, but there are very striking parallels.” (S010, 2022)
- **Historical inevitability (2019):** rejects claims that outcomes were unavoidable when choices remained. “I don’t think things are ever inevitable. If we think that, then we don’t do anything.” (S031, 2019)
- **Historical lessons (2014):** be wary of lessons selected to fit an argument. “I’m always wary of lessons from the past” (S030, 2014)
- **Leadership (2023):** individual leaders matter, but cannot be separated from the forces that shaped them. “we can never, of course, abstract individuals from their own times.” (S003, 2023)
- **Historical context (2017):** history helps open possibilities and ask better questions. “Where history can be useful is in opening our minds to other possibilities.” (S017, 2017)
- **Identity and the past (2009):** historical narratives matter because they are bound up with collective identity. “I think history is a very important part of identity” (S015, 2009)
- **War (2020–2024):** war is an organized social activity, not simply an aberration or isolated battlefield event. “war is a highly organized activity.” (S009, 2024)
- **War's causes (2026):** crises can escalate through accident as well as deliberate intent. “they often break out by accident.” (S001, 2026)
- **Escalation (2026):** leaders trapped by their own commitments face dangerous incentives. “they got themselves into a position where they felt they couldn't back down, and that's always dangerous.” (S001, 2026)
- **International order (2024):** major powers have to find ways to cooperate, even when their aims are narrow. “China and the United States have to find ways they can work together” (S009, 2024)
- **Peace settlements (2022):** the consequences of a war can set conditions for later conflict. “History never repeats itself completely, but there are very striking parallels.” (S010, 2022)
- **Brexit and political leadership (2017):** a negotiator without a strong domestic position is weakened in external bargaining. “she is not in a strong negotiating position.” (S029, 2017)
- **Russia and historical memory (2017):** understand present political choices through historical experience and memory. “To understand the Russia of today it is essential to know that history” (S017, 2017)
- **Historical expertise and prediction (2023):** history offers warnings, not reliable blueprints. “Not looking for clear blueprints and lessons” (S003, 2023)
