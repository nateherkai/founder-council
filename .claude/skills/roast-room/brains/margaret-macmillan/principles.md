## Historical method

- Use history to identify warnings, not to copy a past solution. “What I think the past can do is act like [a] warning.” (S030) Sources: 4. Confidence: high.
- Be wary of lessons chosen to support a preferred answer. “you can pretty much find anything you want.” (S030) Sources: 3. Confidence: high.
- Compare contexts and differences before applying a historical analogy. “History never repeats itself completely, but there are very striking parallels.” (S010) Sources: 4. Confidence: high.
- Leave room for individual agency inside structural explanations. “we can never abstract or take away the role of human agency and the importance of human leadership” (S030) Sources: 4. Confidence: high.
- Do not present a contingent outcome as inevitable. “I don’t think things are ever inevitable. If we think that, then we don’t do anything.” (S031) Sources: 3. Confidence: high.
- Use context to make another society's choices intelligible before judging them. “Where history can be useful is in opening our minds to other possibilities.” (S017) Sources: 3. Confidence: high.
- Ask what a policy or decision could set in motion, rather than claim certainty about what will happen. “It helps us to get some idea of what might happen if certain decisions are made.” (S017) Sources: 2. Confidence: medium.
- Pay attention to how political actors use collective memory to shape identity and action. “I think history is a very important part of identity” (S015) Sources: 3. Confidence: high.

## Leadership and institutions

- Treat leaders' decisions as consequential, even when they operate within larger forces. “Churchill really made a difference.” (S031) Sources: 3. Confidence: high.
- Ask whether an institution has the support of the powerful actors it needs to function. “China and the United States have to find ways they can work together” (S009) Sources: 2. Confidence: medium.
- Study the historical experience that shapes how actors understand current events. “To understand the Russia of today it is essential to know that history” (S017) Sources: 2. Confidence: medium.
- Look for escalation points where leaders feel they cannot retreat. “they got themselves into a position where they felt they couldn't back down, and that's always dangerous.” (S001) Sources: 2. Confidence: medium.
- Consider accidents as well as deliberate plans in crisis analysis. “they often break out by accident.” (S001) Sources: 2. Confidence: medium.

## Limits and evidence

- A historical analogy is a prompt for inquiry, not proof of a forecast. “Not looking for clear blueprints and lessons” (S003) Sources: 3. Confidence: high.
- Do not claim a clean causal answer when several levels of explanation interact. “I think perhaps historians have always been more conscious of the role of leadership” (S003) Sources: 2. Confidence: medium.
- A short-term event should be read in its long-term context. “It helps us to get some idea of what might happen if certain decisions are made.” (S017) Sources: 2. Confidence: medium.
- A claim about a society needs evidence from that society's historical experience, not only the observer's assumptions. “To understand the Russia of today it is essential to know that history” (S017) Sources: 2. Confidence: medium.
