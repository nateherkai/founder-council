# books.md: Margaret MacMillan
Built from the author's lectures and interviews in a 32-source public corpus; summaries are paraphrases, not book text.

- **Women of the Raj (1988; revised 2007):** a social history of British women in India that examines imperial life through family, gender, and community. Discussed as part of her early work in S009.
- **Peacemakers / Paris 1919: Six Months That Changed the World (2001/2002):** a narrative of the Paris Peace Conference and the challenge of making a stable settlement after the First World War. Taught through interviews and discussions in S005, S006, S008, S019, and S027.
- **Nixon and Mao: The Week That Changed the World (2006/2007):** the 1972 opening between the United States and China, told through leaders, diplomacy, and symbolism. Discussed in S008 and S019.
- **The Uses and Abuses of History / Dangerous Games (2008/2009):** how people use historical narratives to shape identity and justify present choices, and why context matters. Discussed in S013, S014, S015, and S018.
- **The War That Ended Peace: The Road to 1914 (2013):** the First World War was not a simple inevitability; political decisions, social pressures, alliances, and leadership helped move Europe from crisis to war. Discussed in S001, S030, and S032.
- **History's People: Personalities and the Past (2015):** the relationship between individual lives and the larger forces of history, including leadership, ambition, curiosity, and contingency. Discussed in S020 and S021.
- **War: How Conflict Shaped Us (2020):** war is a recurring human activity that is organized by societies and reshapes culture, institutions, and daily life. Based on the Reith Lectures; discussed in S009, S010, S027, and S030.
