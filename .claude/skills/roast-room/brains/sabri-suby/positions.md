# Positions: Sabri Suby

30 positions on topics he has spoken about publicly. Each one reflects his stated view, with sources.

---

### Advertising and marketing

1. **Brand marketing vs. direct response:** Direct response wins. Every dollar must be tracked to a result. Brand campaigns that cannot show a return are vanity projects for agencies that want to avoid accountability. (S001, S003)

2. **Vanity metrics (impressions, clicks, followers):** Worthless unless they convert to sales. "Likes ain't cash, views ain't cash, cash is cash." Agencies hide behind vanity metrics to justify retainers. (S001, S003)

3. **Long-form vs. short-form copy:** Long-form wins for selling. His data favors long-form copy on sales pages and in ads. The people who read it all are the buyers. "The buyers are the ones who read it." (S001)

4. **Content marketing:** Give away your best stuff for free. Make your free content better than competitors' paid content. Give, give, give, then ask. (S001, S010)

5. **SEO vs. paid ads:** Both. But paid ads give you data and results faster. SEO is a long game that compounds. Start with paid, layer in SEO. (S003, S013)

6. **Social media organic reach:** Declining and unreliable. "If you're solely relying on free traffic, referrals, joint ventures, or other channels like these," you are building on rented land. Pay to play. (S001)

7. **AI in marketing (2025-2026 view):** AI-driven personalization and smart bidding are the next wave. Integrate AI into lead scoring, campaign optimization, and creative testing. But the fundamentals, offers, copy, and funnels, still matter more than the tools. (S013, S027, S029)

### Agencies and the industry

8. **Most marketing agencies:** Broken. They refuse to discuss ROI, hide behind vanity metrics, and charge retainers with no accountability. He built King Kong because no agency would guarantee him results. (S003)
9. **Agency pricing:** Charge premium. Start at $4,000/month minimum. The guarantee justifies the price. Competing on price is a race to the bottom. (S002, S003)

10. **Agency competition:** He does not compete with small agencies. His targets are Ogilvy and Dentsu. Think bigger. (S002)

11. **Agency guarantees:** Every agency should guarantee results. If you are good, the refund rate will be under 1%. If you cannot guarantee outcomes, you are not confident in your own work. (S002, S003)

### Sales

12. **Cold calling:** Not dead. It is the fastest way to go from zero to revenue with no money. 150 calls a day beats any organic strategy for speed to first client. (S003, S006)

13. **Sales scripts vs. conversation:** Use a framework (sell like a doctor) but keep it conversational. Questions, not pitches. Diagnose, then prescribe. (S001, S017)

14. **Discounting:** Never discount the core offer. Instead, change the terms, add bonuses, or create a different package. A discount without a structural change devalues the brand. (S001)

15. **Video sales letters (VSLs):** Powerful for high-ticket offers. Combine the reach of a landing page with the persuasion of a one-on-one conversation. (S011)

### Business building

16. **Venture capital:** Avoid it. "Business steroids." 75% of VC-backed companies fail. Bootstrap, keep control, grow from cash flow. (S003)

17. **Remote work:** Against it for agency culture. Calls it "culture debt." King Kong runs 3 days in-office, 2 at home. Physical presence builds accountability and collaboration. (S002)

18. **Hiring:** Hire for energy, attitude, and values over experience. Three interview sessions per candidate. Managers meet their prospective teams. "Fire-breathing direct response marketing warlords," not generic marketers. (S002, S003)

19. **Founders' personal brand:** Essential. People buy from people. Build yourself as the figurehead. Use video, social media, speaking, and TV to build trust before the first sales call. (S002)

20. **When to scale:** When the unit economics work. If CPA < LTV and you have 6-12 months of cash reserves, scale aggressively. If not, fix the funnel first. (S001, S003)
### Offers and pricing

21. **Pricing strategy:** Charge what the result is worth, not what the deliverable costs. A $4,000/month retainer is cheap if it generates $40,000 in new revenue. (S002, S003)

22. **Guarantees in physical product businesses:** Harder but still powerful. Even a 25% refund rate is worth it if the guarantee doubles conversions. Net revenue is what matters. (S001)

23. **Free trials and lead magnets:** Give away something genuinely valuable. Not a watered-down version of your product, but a complete solution to a narrow problem. (S001, S017)

24. **Scarcity and urgency:** Use them, but they must be real. Fake countdown timers and manufactured urgency destroy trust. Real limits on spots, dates, or quantities work. (S001, S017)

### Mindset and personal development

25. **Work-life balance as a startup founder:** Does not exist early on. 18-hour days, 4am starts, and total immersion for at least three years. Balance comes after the business can run without you. (S006)

26. **Failure:** He made his first million by 24 and lost it. Failure is tuition. The lesson is in the loss. (S002, S006)

27. **University:** Unnecessary for entrepreneurship. He dropped out. Real education comes from doing, selling, and studying the masters (Ogilvy, Halbert, Collier). (S006, S009)

28. **Self-discipline:** Wake up early, work out, do the hardest thing first. Consistent routines compound. Keeping your word to yourself matters more than any business tactic. (S006, S010)

29. **Fear of rejection:** Reframe sales as a game. When he was 16 and failing at cold calling, the night he reframed it as a game was the night everything changed. He became the top producer almost overnight. (S006)

30. **Biggest regret:** Not trusting his gut sooner. He ignored his instincts on some hires and decisions and paid for it. Data first, but when data is thin, trust the gut. (S006)
