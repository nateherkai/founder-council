# Frameworks: Sabri Suby

18 frameworks, grouped by the job they do (customer research, offers, traffic, conversion, sales, scale). Quotes come from public summaries, interviews, and transcripts.

---

## Customer research

### 1. The Halo Strategy (Sell Like Crazy)
- **What it is:** A research method for finding where your dream buyers hang out online and what they say when nobody is selling to them.
- **Parts:** Mine Facebook Groups, Reddit threads, Quora questions, Amazon reviews, blog comments, and YouTube comment sections. Use AnswerThePublic and Google autocomplete. Capture their exact words, phrases, pains, fears, hopes, and dreams. Build your avatar from what they actually say, not what you assume.
- **When to use:** Before writing any ad, offer, or sales page. Before launching a product or entering a new market.
- **How to score:** Does the research use the customer's exact language? Can you name the forums, groups, and review sites you mined? If you are guessing at the avatar, you have not done the Halo.
- **Quote:** "For your message to stand out from the crowd and really win customers, you must go deeper. Much deeper." (S001)
- **Sources:** 8 (S001, S006, S016, S017, S021, S024, S025, S028)

### 2. Nine Questions to Define Your Dream Buyer (Sell Like Crazy)
- **What it is:** A nine-question framework for building a detailed customer avatar with a name, age, habits, fears, and desires.
- **Parts:** Where do they hang out? Where do they get information? What are their biggest frustrations? What are their hopes and dreams? What are their deepest fears? What makes them happy? What makes them angry? What does a day in their life look like? Who do they trust?
- **When to use:** Before writing any marketing, when entering a new market, or when current marketing is not converting.
- **How to score:** Can you answer all nine in specific, concrete detail? "Hangs out in the Mothers of Melbourne Facebook group" beats "uses social media."
- **Quote:** "Knowing exactly where your dream buyers are hanging out influences a lot of things including where you should advertise, what you should promote, and the tone and wording of your copy." (S001)
- **Sources:** 6 (S001, S016, S017, S024, S025, S028)
### 3. The Larger Market Formula (the 3/17/20/60 pyramid) (Sell Like Crazy)
- **What it is:** Any market splits into four tiers by awareness. Most businesses only target the top 3% and ignore 97% of potential buyers.
- **Parts:** 3% ready to buy now. 17% actively researching solutions. 20% aware of the problem but not looking yet. 60% do not even know they have the problem. Target the 97% with education and value-based content, then move them up.
- **When to use:** Any conversation about market size, targeting, or why ads are expensive and competitive.
- **How to score:** Is the marketing hitting only the 3%? If so, cost per acquisition will be high and scale will be limited. A plan that educates the 97% through a funnel scores higher.
- **Quote:** "Or how about those who don't even know they're thirsty to begin with, because this market is a whopping 60% of all people!" (S001)
- **Sources:** 7 (S001, S016, S017, S024, S025, S028, S013)

### 4. The Power 4% (Sell Like Crazy)
- **What it is:** Apply 80/20 to itself: 4% of customers produce 64% of revenue.
- **Parts:** Identify the top 20% of customers who produce 80% of revenue, then find the top 20% of that group. Build your avatar from them, not from the average customer. Market to attract more of them and spend less time on the 96%.
- **When to use:** Customer segmentation, when deciding who to write ads for, or when revenue is flat despite growing customer count.
- **How to score:** Can you name your Power 4%? Does new marketing look like it would attract them? If you are writing for the average customer, you are diluting the message.
- **Quote:** "I call these customers your Power 4%. Why? Because they have the power to make you filthy rich!" (S001)
- **Sources:** 5 (S001, S016, S017, S024, S025)

## Offers

### 5. The Godfather Strategy (Sell Like Crazy)
- **What it is:** Build an offer so compelling that prospects feel they cannot refuse. Named after The Godfather's "offer he can't refuse."
- **Parts:** Seven components: (1) Rationale, explain why the deal is good and answer "what's the catch." (2) Build value, show full-price worth. (3) Pricing, start accessible, profit on upsells. (4) Payment options, installments. (5) Premiums, bonuses that increase perceived value. (6) Guarantee, reduce buyer risk. (7) Scarcity, real limits on spots, dates, or quantity.
- **When to use:** Designing any offer, pricing a service, writing a sales page, or when close rates are low.
- **How to score:** Count the seven components present. Missing three or more means the offer is weak. If prospects can easily compare your price to competitors, the offer is not a Godfather.
- **Quote:** "your next move is to create the most irresistible bait for your dream buyer." (S001)
- **Sources:** 9 (S001, S006, S016, S017, S024, S025, S010, S028, S013)
### 6. High-Value Content Offer (HVCO) (Sell Like Crazy)
- **What it is:** An ethical bribe: give away genuinely valuable content in exchange for a prospect's name and email.
- **Parts:** Solve one narrow, specific problem. Make it immediately useful. 29 types including checklists, reports, templates, video trainings, and cheat sheets. Use emotional headline formulas: "How to [result] without [pain] in [timeframe]." Keep it short; even two pages can work.
- **When to use:** Lead generation, building an email list, top of funnel, or when cost per lead is too high.
- **How to score:** Does the HVCO solve a real problem the dream buyer has right now? Is it valuable enough that you could charge for it? If it reads like a brochure, it fails.
- **Quote:** "to offer your prospects incredible value, typically in the form of the solution to a problem they're struggling with." (S001)
- **Sources:** 7 (S001, S016, S017, S024, S025, S028, S013)

### 7. The 17-Step Secret Selling System (Sell Like Crazy)
- **What it is:** A sales page structure that walks the prospect from attention to purchase in a specific order.
- **Parts:** Name the audience, big promise headline, subheadline, 5-6 punchy bullets, define the problem, present the solution, features to benefits, proof, third-party validation, bonuses, value stack, cost comparison (daily cost framing), payment plans, guarantee, urgency/scarcity, one clear CTA. A confused mind always says no, so one CTA only.
- **When to use:** Writing any landing page, sales page, or long-form ad.
- **How to score:** Walk through all 17 steps. Any missing step is a leak in the conversion.
- **Sources:** 6 (S001, S016, S017, S024, S025, S028)

### 8. Risk reversal and guarantees (Sell Like Crazy + King Kong model)
- **What it is:** Move risk from the buyer to you. If the result does not happen, the buyer pays nothing.
- **Parts:** Types: unconditional refund, conditional (hit milestones), performance-based (pay only for results), double-your-money-back. King Kong's model: guaranteed results or you do not pay. Under 1% refund rate proves the guarantee works as a conversion tool and a quality filter.
- **When to use:** Close rate problems, price objections, or entering a competitive market where trust is low.
- **How to score:** Does the guarantee shift meaningful risk to you? A vague "satisfaction guarantee" scores low. "Guaranteed Google rankings in 90 days or we work for free" scores high. (S001, S003)
- **Quote:** "I want to have my website ranked higher on Google. Guaranteed Google rankings in 90 days or we work for free!" (S001)
- **Sources:** 8 (S001, S002, S003, S006, S013, S016, S017, S024)
## Traffic and ads

### 9. Unit economics test (Sell Like Crazy)
- **What it is:** Know exactly how much a customer costs and how much they are worth before spending a dollar on ads.
- **Parts:** Three numbers: cost per lead (CPL), cost per acquisition (CPA), and customer lifetime value (LTV). Maximum customer cost = total revenue / number of customers. If CPA < LTV, scale the spend. If every $1 returns $2, the question is not whether to spend but how much.
- **When to use:** Before any ad campaign, before scaling spend, or when marketing "isn't working."
- **How to score:** Can you name all three numbers? If not, you are guessing. A campaign without unit economics is gambling.
- **Quote:** "So, the big question is, how much should you invest in advertising for your business?" (S001)
- **Sources:** 7 (S001, S003, S016, S017, S024, S025, S028)

### 10. Sell the click, not the product (Sell Like Crazy)
- **What it is:** An ad's only job is to get the click. The landing page sells the product. Trying to sell in the ad burns money.
- **Parts:** The ad creates intrigue, not conviction. It speaks to the pain point, hints at a solution, and makes the prospect raise their hand. Long-form copy in the ad is fine if it earns the click. Make ads that look like valuable content, not like ads.
- **When to use:** Writing any ad, reviewing ad performance, or when click-through rates are low.
- **How to score:** Does the ad try to close the sale, or does it earn a click? An ad that lists features and prices is selling. An ad that promises a result and says "here's how" is selling the click.
- **Sources:** 5 (S001, S016, S017, S024, S025)

### 11. Master one channel before adding another (Sell Like Crazy)
- **What it is:** Start with one traffic channel, optimize it until it prints money, then add the next.
- **Parts:** Pick the channel where your dream buyer already hangs out. Run it at volume. Optimize the message, the targeting, and the landing page. Only expand to a second channel after the first is profitable and systemized.
- **When to use:** Any "where should we advertise?" question, or when spend is spread thin across too many channels.
- **How to score:** Is the current channel maxed out and profitable? If not, adding another will dilute focus and budget.
- **Sources:** 5 (S001, S016, S017, S024, S010)

### 12. The Godfather Offer for agencies (performance deal) (My First Million)
- **What it is:** Find businesses already succeeding on one channel, then offer to run a second channel for them with no upfront fee, taking a percentage of the sales it generates.
- **Parts:** No risk for the client. Your fee is a cut of incremental revenue. You prove value before asking for money. Works for agencies, freelancers, and service businesses.
- **When to use:** Entering a new market, winning first clients, or when prospects object to upfront fees.
- **How to score:** Does the offer eliminate financial risk for the buyer? Does it tie your fee to results? If you are charging a flat retainer with no performance link, it is not a Godfather.
- **Sources:** 3 (S010, S002, S003)
## Conversion

### 13. The Magic Lantern Technique (Sell Like Crazy)
- **What it is:** A value-driven email and video nurture sequence that builds trust before the sales ask.
- **Parts:** After capturing a lead, send a series of genuinely valuable emails and videos. Each one solves a small problem, demonstrates expertise, and includes a soft CTA. By the time you pitch, the prospect already trusts you. Address skepticism by giving more value, not by selling harder.
- **When to use:** Email sequences, lead nurturing, or when leads are not converting to sales calls.
- **How to score:** Does the sequence give real value, or is every email a pitch? Count the give-to-ask ratio. If it is not at least 3:1, the magic lantern is broken.
- **Sources:** 6 (S001, S016, S017, S024, S025, S028)

### 14. Sell like a doctor (Sell Like Crazy)
- **What it is:** Diagnose before you prescribe. Ask questions, listen, then recommend.
- **Parts:** Ask open-ended questions to uncover the real problem. Let the prospect talk 90% of the time. Use their own language in your prescription. Present a tailored solution, state the price confidently, then stop talking and wait.
- **When to use:** Sales calls, consultations, closing high-ticket offers, or when reps are pitching instead of listening.
- **How to score:** What percentage of the call does the prospect talk? If the rep talks more than 20%, it is a pitch, not a diagnosis. Tag lost deals by whether the diagnosis step happened.
- **Sources:** 6 (S001, S016, S017, S024, S025, S028)

## Sales

### 15. The 80/20 of selling (Sell Like Crazy)
- **What it is:** 80% of a founder's time should go to sales and marketing. Apply 80/20 again: 4% of activities produce 64% of revenue.
- **Parts:** Identify your revenue-producing activities. Delegate or automate everything else. If you can outsource a task for less than your hourly rate, do it. Selling is not something you do on the side; it is the owner's number one job.
- **When to use:** Time allocation, hiring decisions, or when the founder is buried in operations.
- **How to score:** What percentage of the founder's time goes to revenue-producing activity? Below 50% is a red flag. Below 20% means the business is running the founder, not the other way around.
- **Quote:** "As the owner, your number one responsibility is to sell." (S001)
- **Sources:** 6 (S001, S006, S016, S017, S024, S025)

### 16. Cold calling at volume (the 150-a-day system) (King Kong origin)
- **What it is:** When you have no money, no brand, and no network, outwork everyone with raw volume of outreach.
- **Parts:** 150 cold calls a day. Target businesses already spending on ads but getting poor results. Offer guaranteed results. Land first client within days, not months. Keep calling until revenue funds advertising. This is the bootstrap bridge.
- **When to use:** Starting from zero, no ad budget, no referrals, or when a founder says "I don't have money for marketing."
- **How to score:** Are you making 100+ outreach actions a day? If not, you have not earned the right to say "cold outreach doesn't work." (S003, S006)
- **Quote:** "You simply can't outwork me. Ever." (S006)
- **Sources:** 5 (S003, S006, S009, S013, S014)
## Scale

### 17. One-to-many selling (salesmanship multiplied) (Sell Like Crazy + King Kong)
- **What it is:** Move your best sales pitch from one-to-one conversations into ads, landing pages, videos, and emails that reach thousands.
- **Parts:** Take your best sales script and turn it into copy, video, and automated email sequences. One sales asset can do the work of 5-10 salespeople. Marketing automation nurtures leads before the sales call, so one person handles the volume of a team.
- **When to use:** When the founder is the only closer, when sales are capped by available hours, or when hiring salespeople is too expensive.
- **How to score:** Is the founder still doing all the selling 1:1? If so, revenue has a ceiling at the founder's available hours. Automated selling assets remove that ceiling.
- **Sources:** 5 (S001, S006, S003, S016, S017)

### 18. Cash reserves and reinvestment rule (King Kong)
- **What it is:** Keep 6-12 months of overhead in the bank. Everything above that goes into marketing.
- **Parts:** Draw as little as possible early on. Never raise venture capital if you can avoid it (VC is "business steroids" and 75% of VC-backed companies fail). Pay yourself only enough to survive until the business is worth more than a salary. Once the reserve is set, marketing spend should be the biggest line item.
- **When to use:** Budget setting, fundraising questions, or when a founder wants to cut marketing spend to boost short-term profit.
- **How to score:** Does the business have 6-12 months of cash reserves? If yes, is the surplus going to marketing? If the founder is pulling profit while the marketing budget is starved, the priorities are backwards.
- **Quote:** "You should only have a marketing budget if your marketing isn't working." (S001)
- **Sources:** 5 (S001, S003, S006, S013, S014)
