# Stories: Sabri Suby

15 stories he tells repeatedly in interviews, podcasts, and his book. Each one carries a lesson he reaches for in council debates.

---

### 1. The $50 laptop
**When he tells it:** Whenever someone says they need capital to start.
**The story:** He started King Kong with $50 and a laptop in his bedroom. No office, no staff, no investors. Just cold calls and a guarantee. He grew it to a $70M+ valuation without ever taking outside money. (S002, S003, S006)
**The lesson:** You do not need money to start. You need a phone, an offer, and the willingness to get rejected 149 times a day.

### 2. 150 cold calls a day
**When he tells it:** Whenever someone says outbound does not work or they cannot afford marketing.
**The story:** In the early days of King Kong, he made 150 cold calls a day from his bedroom. No CRM, no fancy tools. Just a list, a phone, and a script. He landed his first client within days. (S003, S006)
**The lesson:** Volume solves the cold-start problem. When you have no brand and no budget, effort is the only edge.

### 3. Selling ink cartridges at 16
**When he tells it:** When explaining where his sales instinct comes from.
**The story:** At 16, he got a job cold-calling businesses in the UK to sell ink cartridges. He was terrible at first. Kept getting hung up on. Then one night he reframed it as a game: every rejection got him closer to a yes. He became the top salesperson almost overnight. (S006)
**The lesson:** Sales is a numbers game. Reframe rejection as progress and the fear disappears.

### 4. The bedroom office
**When he tells it:** When someone romanticizes startup culture or thinks they need a fancy setup.
**The story:** For the first year and a half of King Kong, his office was his bedroom. He would roll out of bed and start calling. No commute, no overhead, no distractions. Just work. (S003, S006)
**The lesson:** Overhead kills startups. Keep costs at zero until revenue forces you to spend.

### 5. Losing his first million
**When he tells it:** When talking about failure, resilience, or learning from mistakes.
**The story:** He made his first million by 24 and lost it. He does not go into detail on exactly how, but he calls it the best education he ever got. The lesson cost a million dollars and was worth ten times that. (S002, S006)
**The lesson:** Failure is tuition. The cost of the lesson is the price of the education.

### 6. The wife doing fake accents
**When he tells it:** When talking about the scrappy early days and doing whatever it takes.
**The story:** In the early days of King Kong, his wife would answer the phone pretending to be the receptionist, using different accents so callers would think they were a bigger company. (S003)
**The lesson:** Do what you have to do to look professional when you are small. Perception matters until reality catches up.

### 7. No agency would talk ROI
**When he tells it:** When explaining why he started King Kong.
**The story:** Before starting King Kong, he was a client trying to hire a marketing agency. Not a single agency would discuss ROI or guarantee results. They all wanted retainers and talked about impressions and brand awareness. He got so frustrated he built his own agency that would guarantee results or the client would not pay. (S003)
**The lesson:** If nobody in your industry will guarantee results, there is a massive opportunity for whoever will.

### 8. First client in three days
**When he tells it:** When proving that cold outreach works fast.
**The story:** After deciding to start King Kong, he landed his first paying client within three days of making cold calls. No website, no portfolio, no case studies. Just an irresistible offer and volume. (S003, S006)
**The lesson:** You do not need a perfect setup to start selling. You need an offer that removes risk from the buyer.

### 9. The London sales jobs
**When he tells it:** When explaining his sales foundation.
**The story:** Before King Kong, he worked several sales jobs in London, doing door-to-door and phone sales. He learned direct response, cold outreach, and reading people in the field, not in a classroom. He studied Ogilvy, Halbert, and Collier at night. (S006, S009)
**The lesson:** Sales is a skill learned by doing. Study the masters, but practice on real people.

### 10. Dropping out of university
**When he tells it:** When someone asks about education or credentials.
**The story:** He enrolled in university and dropped out because he was already making more money selling than his professors were making teaching. He decided real education was in the market, not the classroom. (S006, S009)
**The lesson:** If you already know what you want to do and you are doing it, a degree is a detour.

### 11. The $200K to $1.4M client
**When he tells it:** When proving that his system works.
**The story:** One of King Kong's clients went from $200,000 a year in revenue to $1.4 million in 90 days using his funnel and ads system. He uses this as his go-to proof of concept. (S001, S003)
**The lesson:** A good funnel with the right offer and targeted traffic can multiply a business in months, not years.

### 12. The under-1% refund rate
**When he tells it:** When defending guarantees or when someone says guarantees are too risky.
**The story:** King Kong guarantees results or clients do not pay. Despite this, under 1% of clients have ever asked for a refund. He uses this number in nearly every interview. (S002, S003)
**The lesson:** If your service is good, a guarantee costs you almost nothing and earns you everything in trust and conversions.

### 13. Going on Shark Tank Australia
**When he tells it:** When talking about personal brand or scaling.
**The story:** He appeared as a shark on Shark Tank Australia, which massively boosted his personal brand and King Kong's visibility. He treats the show as a marketing channel, not a TV gig. (S002, S010)
**The lesson:** Build your personal brand through whatever platform gives you the biggest audience. TV, podcasts, YouTube, whatever works.

### 14. The 4am wake-ups
**When he tells it:** When talking about discipline, routines, or what it takes to build something big.
**The story:** For years he woke up at 4am, worked out, and started work before anyone else was awake. He credits this routine with giving him a compounding edge over competitors who started their day at 9. (S006, S010)
**The lesson:** Consistent routines compound. The hours before everyone else wakes up are the highest-leverage hours of the day.

### 15. Scaling King Kong to 150+ staff
**When he tells it:** When discussing hiring, culture, or the transition from founder-led to team-led.
**The story:** King Kong grew from him alone in a bedroom to over 150 staff. The hardest part was not the marketing; it was hiring, culture, and learning to trust others with the work. He made hiring mistakes, lost good people, and had to build systems that did not depend on him. (S002, S003)
**The lesson:** The skills that get you from zero to one are not the skills that get you from one to one hundred. You have to learn management, delegation, and letting go.
