# Positions: Richard H. Thaler

Built from 18 interview, talk, and recap sources, 2013–2026. This limited corpus is not a full survey of Thaler's writing. “No recorded position” means no clear support in these sources.

## Choice and behavior

- **Defaults and follow-through:** Use a useful default to simplify action, then verify the default's quality and results. “So it’s all easy.” (S014; 2019)
- **Nudges:** Preserve options and leave a clear route to choose otherwise. “The idea of a nudge is that you can point somebody in a helpful direction without forcing them to do anything.” (S005; 2019)
- **Consent by inaction:** Failure to opt out is weak evidence that a person considered the decision. “Right? Zero.” (S012; 2021)
- **Habit change:** No universal method recorded; Thaler says economists know little about how habits form or persist. “Economists don’t know much about how people form habits or when they stick and when they break.” (S014; 2019)
- **Sunk costs:** Evaluate the future choice, not the irrecoverable past payment. “You should just decide whether the music is worth the annoyance of the rain.” (S017; 2017)

## Product and customer experience

- **Cancellation and switching:** Do not make exit harder than entry; friction can be harmful even when sign-up is easy. “If you make things harder, I call that sludge.” (S016; 2025)
- **Disclosure:** Make the user-facing choice environment clear; Thaler calls choice architecture central to decision quality. “The choice architecture is everything. The tax subsidy is nothing.” (S008; 2013)
- **Pricing and discounts:** No general startup pricing playbook recorded. A deal frame may prompt purchases a customer would not otherwise make. “People love deals.” (S018; 2017)
- **Hiring:** No full hiring method recorded. Thaler questions job interviews as a reliable predictor in one edited conversation; do not generalize beyond that discussion. (S012; 2021)

## Policy, incentives, and markets

- **Climate:** Nudges can help but need to sit beside correct prices. “You’ve got to get the prices right. And then nudging can help, and every 1 percent helps.” (S012; 2021)
- **Regulation:** Choice-preserving design is useful, but some problems warrant regulation or mandates. (S002; 2022)
- **Government process:** Simplify processes and involve relevant expertise; cuts alone do not prove sludge reduction. “You do what you can to simplify, and hire experts.” (S016; 2025)
- **Market efficiency:** Perfect market efficiency is not tenable; models should incorporate human behavior. “The idea that markets work perfectly is no longer tenable.” (S017; 2017)
- **AI, remote work, hours, burnout, equity, fundraising, debt, company sale, culture, and competitive strategy:** No recorded position in this corpus.
