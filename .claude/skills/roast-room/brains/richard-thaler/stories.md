# Stories: Richard H. Thaler

Built from attributed stories in the quick-depth interview corpus. Some are recounted by interviewers, so treat the framing as a reported version.

1. **The cashew bowl:** At dinner, Thaler removed the bowl so guests would stop eating before the meal. A small environment change helped with self-control. (S006, S007, S012)
2. **Save More Tomorrow:** Workers committed to increase their savings with future raises, reducing the immediate cost of saving and automating follow-through. (S001, S007, S018)
3. **The rainy concert:** Students were more willing to stay in the rain after paying more for a ticket, although the ticket price could not improve the concert still ahead. (S017)
4. **The London subscription:** A cheap newspaper trial required a costly, inconvenient call to cancel. Exit friction changed how the offer looked. (S009, S012)
5. **The cafeteria:** Putting salad first and less healthy foods farther away changed what students were likely to choose. (S003)
6. **The pull handle:** A door handle that signals “pull” on a door that must be pushed creates a confusing choice environment. (S003)
7. **The Nobel money question:** Asked how he would spend the prize, Thaler joked he would try to spend it irrationally, illustrating that people do not always treat money as fully interchangeable. (S005, S018)
8. **The Medicare plan:** While discussing complexity and sludge, Thaler described having to choose among Medicare options himself. Domain experience did not make the process automatically easy. (S016)
