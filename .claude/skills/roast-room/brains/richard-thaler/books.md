# Books: Richard H. Thaler

Built from interviews and event recaps in the quick-depth corpus. These summaries describe public discussions of the books; no book text was used.

## Misbehaving: The Making of Behavioral Economics (2015)

Thaler's account of the development of behavioral economics, from early anomalies that contradicted standard assumptions to the research program that brought psychological realism into economics. He often uses the cashew story, market anomalies, self-control, and mental accounting to show why models should describe actual people. Discussed in S004, S006, S007, S012, S017.

## Nudge: Improving Decisions About Health, Wealth, and Happiness (2008; final edition 2021, with Cass R. Sunstein)

The book applies choice architecture to decisions about saving, health, and public policy. A nudge changes the environment in a predictable way while keeping options available; defaults and simpler processes are recurring applications. Thaler emphasizes that choice architecture is unavoidable and that a nudge should not be mistaken for a complete solution to structural problems. Discussed in S001, S002, S005, S006, S010–S014.

## The Winner's Curse: Behavioral Economics Anomalies, Then and Now (2025, with Alex Imas)

The corpus includes Thaler discussing a new edition that revisits economic anomalies and considers how the research has developed. It does not contain enough direct discussion to map the book's chapters. Discussed in S004 and S013.
