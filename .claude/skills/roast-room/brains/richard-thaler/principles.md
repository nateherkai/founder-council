# Principles: Richard H. Thaler

Built from 18 public sources in a quick-depth corpus. Confidence is about repeat support in this limited corpus, not a full review of his scholarship.

1. **Describe behavior before prescribing a fix.** Models should represent actual human behavior. Quote: “The idea that markets work perfectly is no longer tenable.” (S017). Support: 3 sources (S008, S017, S018). Confidence: high.
2. **Treat wording and defaults as testable.** Do not dismiss a factor because theory says it should not matter. Quote: “I coined the phrase supposedly irrelevant factors for the kinds of things that economists are sure don't matter, like the way a letter is worded or what the default option is.” (S017). Support: 2 sources (S001, S017). Confidence: medium.
3. **Make wanted actions easy.** Reduce steps and confusion in the path. Quote: “Nudges typically work by making something easy, like automatically signing you up for the retirement plan.” (S014). Support: 5 sources (S001, S006, S012, S013, S014). Confidence: high.
4. **Keep a nudge easy to avoid.** Preserve an ordinary route to a different choice. Quote: “The idea of a nudge is that you can point somebody in a helpful direction without forcing them to do anything.” (S005). Support: 4 sources (S001, S006, S012, S013). Confidence: high.
5. **Look for sludge on both sides of a transaction.** Easy signup and hard cancellation is a warning sign. Quote: “If you make things harder, I call that sludge.” (S016). Support: 5 sources. Confidence: high.
6. **Do not mistake no response for deliberate preference.** Inattention can explain failure to opt out. Quote: “If he failed to fill out some form, how confident would you be that that omission was based on careful thought? Right? Zero.” (S012). Support: 2 sources. Confidence: medium.
7. **Pair a nudge with the right price or rule when needed.** Behavioral design cannot carry the full weight of structural problems. Quote: “You’ve got to get the prices right. And then nudging can help, and every 1 percent helps.” (S012). Support: 4 sources. Confidence: high.
8. **Use observed results, not the elegance of an idea, to judge a policy.** Quote: “What follows from there? How should we do things differently if people aren't perfect?” (S017). Support: 3 sources. Confidence: high.
9. **Ask the relevant discipline for help.** Thaler says economists know little about habit formation. Quote: “Economists don’t know much about how people form habits or when they stick and when they break.” (S014). Support: 1 source. Confidence: low.
10. **Ignore sunk costs in the next choice.** Quote: “You should just decide whether the music is worth the annoyance of the rain.” (S017). Support: 2 sources (S010, S017). Confidence: medium.
11. **Prefer practical improvement to waiting for a complete solution.** Quote: “And in each case, we should do what we can to make incremental changes.” (S013). Support: 2 sources. Confidence: medium.
12. **Check that the default itself is good.** Enrollment can go up while contributions or outcomes remain poor. Quote: “So it’s all easy.” (S014). Support: 2 sources. Confidence: medium.
