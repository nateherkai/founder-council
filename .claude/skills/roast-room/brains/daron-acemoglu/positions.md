# Positions: Daron Acemoglu

Built from interview and lecture transcripts plus public paper records. These are his documented positions, not a general economics template. Dates follow the dated source when available.

## Technology, labor, and distribution

- **Growth and wages:** GDP growth does not guarantee broad wage gains. In the postwar United States, wages and output rose together; other periods diverged. "there's a variety of evidence suggesting that this doesn't always work so seamlessly." (S004, 2023)
- **AI and aggregate productivity:** His 2024 estimate is modest, conditional on the task-level evidence and assumptions used; he warns hard-to-learn tasks could make early estimates overstate gains. "These macroeconomic effects appear nontrivial but modest" (S024, 2024)
- **AI and inequality:** AI could widen the gap between capital income and labor income even without uniformly reducing wages. (S024, 2024)
- **AI at work:** Favor development that complements expertise and creates new tasks, instead of treating automation as the only path. "we want them to be complements to humans." (S010, 2024)
- **Automation:** It can displace workers even while increasing output per worker. Assess labor share, demand, and new tasks separately. "Automation always reduces the labor share in value added and may reduce labor demand even as it raises productivity." (S014, 2019)
- **New tasks:** Technology that creates new work can reinstate labor demand. "What new tasks do is that they expand what they do." (S002, 2026)
- **Task-based analysis:** Map work by task, skill, and capital. Effects differ by type of technological change. "Production in each industry requires the completion of various tasks that can be assigned to workers with different skills or to capital." (S025, 2024)
- **Inequality and jobs:** Worker outcomes depend on how institutions and technology jointly distribute bargaining power and opportunities. (S004, 2023; S021, 2021)
- **Technology measurement:** Current evidence and measurement remain weak, especially for AI adoption. "We're not doing a great job in general in the economic profession of measuring technologies." (S004, 2023)
- **AI forecasts:** Avoid treating exposure as a prediction of realized automation or GDP impact. (S024, 2024)

## Institutions, growth, and policy

- **Institutions and prosperity:** Institutional arrangements affect economic incentives and outcomes. "Economic institutions determine the incentives of and the constraints on economic actors, and shape economic outcomes." (S015, 2004)
- **Democracy and growth:** His empirical work finds democracy raises GDP in the long run, with channels including investment, education, reform, public goods, and reduced unrest. (S018, 2014)
- **Education and democratization:** He and coauthors found no evidence that within-country increases in education cause democratization after addressing cross-country correlation. (S029, 2005)
- **Political power:** Formal political authority and practical power through resources, lobbying, or organization both matter. (S001, undated; S017, 2006)
- **Institutional change:** Historical influence persists but does not determine outcomes. Collective action and agency can redirect institutional trajectories. "There’s a lot of agency there—not individual agency, but collective agency." (S007, 2025)
- **State and society:** Effective public capacity and social checks must develop together; the balance varies by country and period. (S007, 2025; S030, 2013)
- **Development history:** Geography, culture, and colonial background alone do not settle the explanation; compare cases and trace mechanisms. "You need something more than just a correlation" (S001, 2025)
- **Local reform:** Institutional reform should be bottom-up and attentive to local political and historical context. "I think it’s bottom-up building." (S001, 2025)
- **Corruption and governance:** Corruption reduces institutional trust and constrains shared prosperity; improve accountability and openness while recognizing reform's political context. (S001, 2025)
- **Policy effectiveness:** Work through mechanisms such as incentives, accountability, worker voice, and bargaining, rather than asserting a policy outcome without evidence. (S001, S004)
