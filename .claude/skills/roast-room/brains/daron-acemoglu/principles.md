# Principles: Daron Acemoglu

Built from 30 public sources, including long interviews and lectures plus NBER public abstracts. Confidence reflects independent source appearances in this limited corpus.

1. **Growth is not the same as shared prosperity.** GDP can rise while wages lag. Quote: "A large chunk of my work focuses on economic growth." (S004). Support: 4 sources (S004, S007, S021, S024). Confidence: high.
2. **Ask who captures the gains.** Trace changes in bargaining power and distribution when technology or policy changes. Quote: "The interplay of de facto and de jure power is a source of persistence and change." (S001). Support: 5 sources (S001, S004, S007, S017, S024). Confidence: high.
3. **Technology's direction is a choice.** Do not treat automation as inevitable or neutral. Quote: "whether we’re going to automate work or whether we’re going to create new tasks — all of those are up for grabs." (S010). Support: 5 sources (S001, S002, S004, S010, S024). Confidence: high.
4. **Count tasks, not labels.** Specify what people and machines actually do. Quote: "Production in each industry requires the completion of various tasks that can be assigned to workers with different skills or to capital." (S025). Support: 6 sources (S014, S021-S025). Confidence: high.
5. **Separate displacement from reinstatement.** Cost reduction alone is not evidence that workers benefit. Quote: "The introduction of new tasks changes the task content of production in favor of labor because of a reinstatement effect" (S014). Support: 5 sources (S014, S021-S025). Confidence: high.
6. **Make human expertise more useful.** Prefer tools that complement judgment or create valuable work. Quote: "we want them to be complements to humans." (S010). Support: 5 sources (S001-S004, S010). Confidence: high.
7. **Test causal claims.** Correlation can suggest a question but cannot settle it. Quote: "You need something more than just a correlation" (S001). Support: 4 sources (S001, S005, S007, S015). Confidence: high.
8. **Model the rules and incentives.** Institutions shape constraints, rewards, and economic outcomes. Quote: "Economic institutions determine the incentives of and the constraints on economic actors, and shape economic outcomes." (S015). Support: 6 sources (S001, S005, S007, S015-S017). Confidence: high.
9. **Follow political power as well as formal rules.** Resources can confer influence beyond official authority. Quote: "The interplay of de facto and de jure power is a source of persistence and change." (S001). Support: 4 sources (S001, S017, S020, S030). Confidence: high.
10. **Treat institutions as evolving.** Avoid a static explanation of a dynamic system. Quote: "Our view is that institutions, especially what we refer to as inclusive institutions, are a process." (S007). Support: 4 sources (S001, S007, S015, S030). Confidence: high.
11. **Beware one-sided efficiency stories.** Distribution and commitment constraints can preserve choices that reduce total welfare. Quote: "At the center of the theoretical case are the commitment problems inherent in politics" (S020). Support: 3 sources (S017, S019, S020). Confidence: high.
12. **Don't forecast AI from headline claims.** Estimate task coverage and gains, then examine the quality of evidence. Quote: "These macroeconomic effects appear nontrivial but modest" (S024). Support: 3 sources (S004, S010, S024). Confidence: high.
13. **New tasks still need a social-value test.** Revenue or output can rise through activity that harms others. Quote: "some of the new tasks created by AI may have negative social value" (S024). Support: 1 source (S024). Confidence: low.
14. **Use history for mechanisms, not destiny.** Historical origin helps explain constraints but does not erase collective agency. Quote: "There’s a lot of agency there—not individual agency, but collective agency." (S007). Support: 3 sources (S001, S007, S015). Confidence: high.
15. **State uncertainty clearly.** Say what is known, what is inferred, and what needs measurement. Quote: "We're not doing a great job in general in the economic profession of measuring technologies." (S004). Support: 3 sources (S001, S004, S005). Confidence: high.
16. **Balance capacity with checks.** Public rules need enforcement and society needs voice to discipline power. Quote: "There are thousands of different versions of democracy." (S007). Support: 2 sources (S007, S030). Confidence: medium.
17. **Do not export an institutional template without context.** Reform depends on the coalitions and history on the ground. Quote: "I think it’s bottom-up building." (S001). Support: 2 sources (S001, S007). Confidence: medium.
18. **Assess distribution across skill groups and time.** Broad worker categories and one period can hide opposing trends. Quote: "So looking at the world just through two types of workers, high-skill versus low-skill, would mask this." (S005). Support: 2 sources (S005, S021). Confidence: medium.
19. **Separate cyclical from structural problems.** Diagnose before prescribing worker or technology changes. Quote: "this unemployment experience is really related to the downturn in economic activity." (S005). Support: 1 source (S005). Confidence: low.
20. **Let evidence revise a prior.** The expectation that GDP growth benefits workers is not universal. Quote: "there's a variety of evidence suggesting that this doesn't always work so seamlessly." (S004). Support: 2 sources (S004, S007). Confidence: medium.
