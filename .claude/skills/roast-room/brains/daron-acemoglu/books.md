# Books: Daron Acemoglu

Built from his interviews and discussions of his books. The corpus contains no book text.

## Economic Origins of Dictatorship and Democracy (2006)
With James A. Robinson. Explains political transitions and institutional arrangements through conflict among groups with different resources and political power. Key lens: distributional conflict, commitment, and changing coalitions. Taught in interviews and the linked research records (S005, S020, S030).

## Why Nations Fail: The Origins of Power, Prosperity, and Poverty (2012)
With James A. Robinson. Argues that economic and political institutions help explain why some societies generate broad prosperity while others concentrate gains. Institutions are made by political choices and can be extractive or more inclusive. Taught in interviews (S005, S006, S007, S010).

## The Narrow Corridor: States, Societies, and the Fate of Liberty (2019)
With James A. Robinson. Explores how liberty depends on a balance between state capacity and social power, with institutions built and maintained through ongoing contest and participation. Taught in interviews (S005, S007).

## Power and Progress: Our Thousand-Year Struggle Over Technology and Prosperity (2023)
With Simon Johnson. Argues that technological advance does not automatically benefit workers or society broadly. Institutions and choices about the direction of innovation shape who has power and who shares in the gains. Taught in interviews and the AI paper (S004, S007, S010, S024).
