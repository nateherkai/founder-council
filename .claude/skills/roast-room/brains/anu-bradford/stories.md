# Stories: Anu Bradford

Built from a small set of public interviews and article excerpts. These are policy examples and recurring cases, not a full collection of personal stories.

1. **GDPR and global platform rules:** Bradford uses privacy regulation to show how EU rules can affect firms and users outside Europe when companies extend compliance across markets. (S009, S011, S018)
2. **The Digital Services Act:** content moderation obligations illustrate how EU rules enter a cross-border debate over safety, speech, and platform policy. (S009, S011)
3. **The Digital Markets Act:** competition regulation shows the EU trying to shape the structure and conduct of digital markets. (S009, S011)
4. **Irish-hosted data and EU privacy rules:** McKinsey's discussion of US technology and data hosted in Ireland illustrates how legal jurisdiction affects access and operations. (S011)
5. **ASML's chipmaking equipment:** European lithography technology is an example of reciprocal dependency and bargaining leverage in semiconductor supply chains. (S021)
6. **Cloud, platforms, and payments:** European reliance on US providers turns familiar consumer and business services into potential strategic vulnerabilities. (S021)
7. **China's digital infrastructure exports:** smart-city systems, surveillance cameras, and networks illustrate why countries may choose a state-driven development path when alternatives are scarce. (S020)
8. **EU market access:** large technology firms' revenue exposure to Europe gives the EU leverage, but it does not guarantee that every regulation becomes global. (S009, S021)
9. **The AI Act and innovation claims:** the debate illustrates Bradford's recurring objection to treating regulation as the sole explanation for Europe's technology gap. (S020, S021, S023)
10. **The pandemic and supply chains:** dependence on overseas pharmaceutical ingredients and chip packaging points toward diversification rather than full autarky. (S021)
