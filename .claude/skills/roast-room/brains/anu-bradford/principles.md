# Principles: Anu Bradford

Built from the available excerpts and interviews. Confidence reflects distinct source records in this small corpus, not the full body of Bradford's work.

1. Regulation and innovation need not be a zero-sum choice. Quote: "Se puede crecer, innovar y proteger los derechos de los ciudadanos al mismo tiempo." (S020). **Sources:** 6 (S011, S013, S020, S021, S022, S023). **Confidence:** high.
2. Diagnose ecosystem weaknesses before treating regulation as the cause of weak innovation. Quote: "Es necesario integrar el mercado digital europeo, crear una unión de los mercados de capitales." (S020). **Sources:** 4 (S020, S021, S022, S023). **Confidence:** high.
3. The Brussels Effect depends on firm incentives and market structure; it is not automatic. Quote: "they want to avoid the cost of complying with multiple different regulatory regimes." (S009). **Sources:** 4 (S007, S009, S018, S024). **Confidence:** high.
4. Rules matter when they are enforced and change real market outcomes. Quote: "Victory is hollow if the EU cannot actually entrench regulations into concrete market outcomes." (S013). **Sources:** 2 (S013, S021). **Confidence:** medium.
5. Policy uncertainty raises the cost of investment because firms cannot plan around shifting rules. Quote: "El mayor arancel de todos es la incertidumbre." (S020). **Sources:** 1 (S020). **Confidence:** low.
6. Private technology firms do not automatically act in the public interest. Quote: "Y como su motivación es el beneficio, no responden al interés público." (S020). **Sources:** 2 (S020, S011). **Confidence:** medium.
7. Regulatory sovereignty can defend values and serve as international leverage. Quote: "So regulatory sovereignty is an aspect of sovereignty that is your ability to write your own laws that reflect your own values." (S021). **Sources:** 3 (S018, S020, S021). **Confidence:** high.
8. Build resilience through prioritized capabilities and alternatives, not a promise of complete self-sufficiency. Quote: "And in that sense this idea of the complete technological sovereignty is an illusion." (S021). **Sources:** 2 (S020, S021). **Confidence:** medium.
9. Large markets give regulators and firms bargaining power, but the leverage must be used deliberately. Quote: "For many of these tech giants, it's 20, 25% of their revenue." (S021). **Sources:** 2 (S009, S021). **Confidence:** medium.
10. Digital rules express competing values about the role of markets, states, rights, and technology. Quote: "We have the American market-driven model, the Chinese state-driven model, and the European rights-driven model." (S011). **Sources:** 5 (S011, S012, S014, S020, S023). **Confidence:** high.
11. Cross-border regulation invites political responses, especially when rules affect powerful foreign firms. **Inferred from S009, S020, S021, S023.**
12. A technology strategy should rank vulnerabilities by their cost and importance, rather than pursue sovereignty for every layer. Quote: "So I think those are the domains where we need to start setting priorities." (S021). **Sources:** 1 (S021). **Confidence:** low.
