## 1. The Brussels Effect

EU rules can become de facto global standards when market access matters and firms choose to extend compliance beyond Europe. Bradford's 2013 framework describes a conditional mechanism: market size and regulatory capacity meet firm-level incentives and the difficulty or expense of maintaining separate production or compliance systems. **Use it** to assess whether a law can shape global products, policies, or market access. **Decision guidance:** ask for affected market share, localization cost, legal exposure, and evidence of actual global adoption; do not assume diffusion. Quote: "they want to avoid the cost of complying with multiple different regulatory regimes." (S009). **Seen in 4 records:** S007, S009, S018, S024.

## 2. Three digital empires

Compare the US market-driven model, China's state-driven model, and the EU's rights-driven model. These are ideal types rooted in different histories and political values, not exhaustive country descriptions. **Use it** to surface conflicts over speech, privacy, competition, state control, and who governs technology. **Decision guidance:** identify which model's constraints and benefits the business is adopting, and test for cross-border conflict. Quote: "We have the American market-driven model, the Chinese state-driven model, and the European rights-driven model." (S011). **Seen in 5 records:** S011, S012, S014, S020, S023.

## 3. Europe’s digital constitution

Treat the EU's accumulated digital rules as a value settlement that embeds rights, democracy, fairness, and redistribution into the digital economy. Bradford gives a normative defense and also asks whether this settlement is coherent, legitimate, and fit for tech rivalry. **Use it** when assessing a product or market strategy that touches privacy, content, competition, or AI. **Decision guidance:** list the rights and public interests the rule protects, then identify burdens and implementation weaknesses. Quote: "The EU has really taken the role of the referee in the digital economy." (S011). **Seen in 2 records:** S018, S021.

## 4. Four technology ecosystem constraints (unnamed)

Bradford attributes Europe's tech gap to more than regulation: an incomplete single market, shallow or fragmented capital markets, legal and cultural barriers to risk-taking, and difficulty attracting global talent. **Use it** to diagnose a competitiveness claim. **Decision guidance:** compare the size of each constraint and identify a measurable reform or company-level response before recommending deregulation. Quote: "Es necesario integrar el mercado digital europeo, crear una unión de los mercados de capitales." (S020). **Seen in 4 records:** S020, S021, S022, S023.

## 5. Selective technological sovereignty (unnamed)

Total self-sufficiency is unrealistic. Map dependencies, rank them by severity and criticality, use reciprocal leverage, and diversify through trusted partners. **Use it** for supply-chain, cloud, payment, AI, defense, or other strategic technology choices. **Decision guidance:** prioritize by cost of disruption and substitutability, not by political symbolism. Quote: "And in that sense this idea of the complete technological sovereignty is an illusion." (S021). **Seen in 2 records:** S020, S021.

## 6. Regulatory sovereignty

The capacity to write laws that express a society's values is itself a source of power. That power can be challenged or politicized when regulation affects foreign firms and rival governments. **Use it** to see beyond compliance costs to questions of who sets the governing rules. **Decision guidance:** assess legal authority, legitimacy, enforcement, and likely political response. Quote: "So regulatory sovereignty is an aspect of sovereignty that is your ability to write your own laws that reflect your own values." (S021). **Seen in 2 records:** S021, S023.

## 7. From rule to market outcome (unnamed)

Regulatory influence should be judged by implementation and changes in firm conduct, not only by passing a law. **Use it** to distinguish symbolic rulemaking from durable governance. **Decision guidance:** define the observable market outcome and enforcement milestones. Quote: "Victory is hollow if the EU cannot actually entrench regulations into concrete market outcomes." (S013). **Seen in 2 records:** S013, S021.

## 8. Whole-of-government economic security (unnamed)

Trade, defense, technology, and economic security can interact, so a strategy organized in isolated policy silos may miss coercion or dependencies. **Use it** for decisions that span several jurisdictions or critical sectors. **Decision guidance:** identify which functions must coordinate and which dependencies could be weaponized. Quote: "all of this is part of the weaponization agenda." (S021). **Seen in 2 records:** S020, S021.
